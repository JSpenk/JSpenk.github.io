Quantum Key Distribution Demo

A simplified, browser-native walkthrough of my E91 QKD simulator.

i
The goal, plainly: E91 lets two parties (Alice and Bob) agree on a shared secret encryption key over a public channel — and unlike most encryption, an eavesdropper isn't just "hard to detect," they're provably detectable, because listening in on quantum-entangled particles physically disturbs the correlation between them. That disturbance shows up as two measurable numbers: QBER (how many key bits came out wrong) and the CHSH value (how strongly Alice and Bob's results violate what's classically possible — the whole basis of the security proof). This demo reimplements that core statistics engine directly in JavaScript, using the same correlation math (based on measurement-angle differences) as my Python/Qiskit simulator — it is not Qiskit running in your browser, just the same underlying formula.
